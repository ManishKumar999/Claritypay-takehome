# Notes

## Assumptions

- I use **2026-06-22** as the reporting date throughout. An A1 installment is
  settled when the recorded payment date is on or before that date and the paid
  amount covers the amount due. Late payments therefore reduce the balance once
  paid; partial and unpaid installments do not. Running paid uses contractual
  `amount_due_usd`, as requested, and excludes late fees.
- The sum of installment amounts differs from `total_repayable_usd` for 4,890
  plans by at most $0.06, consistent with per-installment rounding. I preserve
  the unadjusted running paid amount and set only the final balance of a complete,
  fully settled plan to zero when the residual is within the observed $0.06.
- For A2, a due installment is any status other than `scheduled`; delinquent
  means `late`, `missed`, or `written_off`, exactly as defined. Rates are ranked
  before display rounding. `DENSE_RANK` retains all merchants tied at any of the
  three worst distinct rates.
- A3 assigns collections to `paid_date` and sums actual `paid_amount_usd`,
  including late or partial receipts. Empty calendar months are explicitly zero
  so `LAG` compares consecutive months. The first-month change and change from a
  zero prior month are `NULL`; June 2026 is partial through June 22.
- A4 uses the count-based definition in the brief: fully paid on-time
  installments divided by due installments. Only customers with due history
  enter `NTILE`; all customers remain in the output. Customers without plans are
  labelled `No loan originated`, while customers with plans but nothing due are
  labelled `No due installments`; both have a `NULL` rate. Equal rates are
  ordered by total dollars due, then customer ID, for reproducibility.
- Account links are treated as undirected because both directions are supplied.
  B1 and B4 exclude source account 700 from counts. Equal-cost paths prefer fewer
  hops and then the lexicographically smaller path. A link is investigative
  evidence under the supplied weights, not proof of fraud.

## A trade-off I made

For A4 I prioritized four similarly sized, reproducible segments using `NTILE`.
This can split customers with the same on-time rate at a boundary. Using total
dollars due as a secondary ordering rewards a rate supported by greater exposure,
but it does not make the underlying repayment rates different. In production I
would show the continuous rate and observation count alongside the segment and
avoid using a quartile alone for an adverse decision.

## C4 extension: cohort repayment curve

I grouped customers by signup quarter and assigned each due installment to the
number of whole months between signup and due date. For each cohort-age pair, the
curve shows the count-weighted proportion of installments fully paid by their due
date. Hover text includes the on-time and due counts because newer cohorts and
later ages have fewer observations. Cohorts should be compared only at shared
account ages; a shorter line usually reflects limited maturity, not better or
worse performance. The deliverable is `figures/cohort_repayment.html` and is
generated by `python/extensions.py`.

## What I would do with more time

1. Review the payment definitions with Product and Risk, especially how partial
   payments, late-but-paid installments, and customers without due history should
   be treated in operational reporting.
2. Investigate why the highest-delinquency merchants perform poorly by comparing
   their customer risk bands, products, regions, and origination cohorts. This
   would help distinguish merchant-specific issues from differences in customer
   mix.
3. Add drill-downs from the dashboard to the underlying plans and installments,
   so an analyst can move from a portfolio signal to the records driving it and
   validate the result before taking action.

## Reflection

Account 700 connects directly to 2800 in one hop through a shared email domain
(distance 20), but the three-hop device/card/device chain has total distance 4.
This illustrates why connection proximity and evidence strength answer different
questions and why the fraud team should review the underlying signals rather than
rely on hop count alone.
